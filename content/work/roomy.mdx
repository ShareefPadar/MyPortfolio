## The Problem — Research by Living It

Co-living in high-density cities like Dubai creates a logistical disaster at scale. I wasn't observing this problem from the outside — I was living inside it, in a 21-person shared apartment. Rent chased through WhatsApp groups. Cash handoffs with no receipts. One laundry machine, no booking system. Disputes with no paper trail.

Being embedded with my users meant research wasn't a phase — it was daily life. And it showed me where the arguments actually started: not with the money itself, but with **missing shared truth**. Nobody could see who had paid, who owed what, or who booked the machine.

The core failure: **no shared system of record** for money, facilities, or accountability.

## Designing for Two Roles

The same product serves two very different users, so I designed two interfaces on one codebase:

- **The admin** — the flat lead collecting rent — needs the full picture: a ledger across 21 tenants, payment confirmation, arrears tracking. Their success metric is chasing less.
- **Members** need exactly one number — *"what do I owe?"* — plus proof of what they've paid and a fair shot at the washing machine.

Members see only their own balance; the admin sees everyone's. That scoping was the core design decision, not a technical one: most trust problems in shared living are really **visibility problems**.

## Key Features

### Smart Rent Ledger
The ledger auto-calculates each tenant's balance from their room type, shared utility split, and any outstanding arrears from previous months. Payment status is one tap to confirm for the admin, and always visible to the member it belongs to — no more screenshot wars in the group chat.

### Live-Status Laundry Scheduler
Real-time machine status with hard daily limits (max 2 bookings per tenant per day). Conflicts are prevented at the data layer — not argued about in the corridor.

## The Architecture, Briefly

To make every interaction feel instant, I built a pseudo-relational database engine on top of `localStorage`: entities with foreign-key-style relations, atomic multi-entity transactions (confirming a payment updates the ledger, the tenant balance, and the admin summary in one operation), and live queries that re-render the UI when data changes. No network, no spinner — every tap responds in under 16ms.

## What I Learned About AI-Assisted Building

This project was my deepest experiment with AI-assisted development using Cursor: AI for boilerplate, state sync, and edge-case hunting — me for the data model, the role system, and the conflict-resolution logic. The lesson that stuck: **AI accelerates execution but cannot replace systems thinking.** I had to understand why relational databases work before I could fake one convincingly without a backend.
